# Emails and the cover letter

## Register

Academic English correspondence is **warm, brief, and flat in status**. Korean-style
deference translated literally reads as either obsequious or odd.

**No flattery.** Do not tell a PI they are world-renowned, eminent, or that their work is
groundbreaking. After an apology from them it reads as insincere; before a request it
reads as softening-up. Worse, it emphasises the status gap, which weakens the candidate's
position — they want to be read as a junior colleague, not a supplicant.

If warmth is wanted, attach it to a **circumstance**, not to the person:

- No: *"Especially as a world-renowned scientist, you must be very busy."*
- Yes: *"I completely understand how demanding a schedule like yours can be."*

**No apologising for existing.** Not *"I'm sorry to bother you"*, not *"I know you're very
busy, but"*. Open with the substance.

## Time zones — get this exactly right

Errors here are the most common and the most embarrassing.

- Write the **recipient's zone first**, the candidate's in parentheses.
- Always label the **day offset**: `6:00–8:00 pm EDT (7:00–9:00 am KST, next day)`.
- `9-12pm` is not valid notation. Write `9:00 pm – 12:00 am`.
- When a list of offered slots straddles midnight in one zone, **several slots will carry
  the same date in that zone**. Never reply with a bare date — name the slot in the
  sender's own zone and repeat the converted time. Ambiguity here costs another round trip,
  and after a missed meeting another round trip is expensive.

When choosing among offered slots, weigh in this order: (1) the PI's likelihood of
actually attending — mid-morning beats end-of-day, and a slot adjacent to their evening is
where double-bookings happen; (2) the candidate's alertness; (3) preparation time.

## A missed meeting

If the PI does not appear: wait ten minutes in the room, then email once — short, no
reproach, offering to reschedule. If they apologise, accept it in one sentence, do not
dwell, and move immediately to logistics. Nothing is gained by making them feel worse,
and the next email should feel like a fresh start.

Always close a rescheduling email by asking for a calendar invitation with the link.

## The cover letter

Four beats, one paragraph each:

1. **What I work on and what I established** — the candidate's own result, stated as a
   finding, not as a technique.
2. **Why this lab specifically** — anchored to a *specific claim in a specific paper*,
   including the PI's own first-author work where relevant. Generic admiration is worse
   than nothing.
3. **What I would want to work on** — phrased as a question the lab has left open, drawn
   from the gap map. Not as an offer of services.
4. **Logistics** — availability, funding status, references.

Do **not** devote a paragraph to how the candidate's prior system could be applied there.
See the skill's primary rule.

## The recommendation-letter conversation

Sometimes the candidate cannot obtain a letter from their primary advisor, or the
situation is complicated — early graduation, a project reassignment, a strained
relationship. This will have to be said eventually; saying it badly is worse than saying
it late.

Principles:

- **Never characterise the advisor negatively.** Not once, not mildly. It transfers
  entirely onto the candidate and tells the PI how the candidate will one day describe them.
- **State the structural fact, not the feeling.** Lab circumstances, project allocation,
  and timing are facts. Resentment is not.
- **Lead with who *will* write.** Name the strong letters first; the gap becomes a
  footnote rather than the headline.
- **Offer a substitute of comparable standing** — a co-advisor, a collaborating PI, a
  thesis committee member who knows the work directly.
- **Have tiers ready**: a one-sentence version for a passing mention, a short paragraph if
  asked, and a fuller account if pressed. Prepare all three; use the shortest that fits.

Draft the request email to the advisor separately, and keep it free of anything that reads
as a complaint — it may be forwarded.
